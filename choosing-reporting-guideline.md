# Choosing the Right Reporting Guideline for Authors and Editorial Staff

Manuscripts often arrive at editorial offices with the wrong reporting checklist attached, or with no checklist at all. The result can be an avoidable round of revisions before peer review.

The first step is to identify the study design or review type and then select the reporting guideline that applies to it. Some designs also have specific extensions. The target journal may have additional requirements.

The tables and worksheet below cover common guidelines and provide a way to check the selected checklist against the manuscript. They are not a substitute for the current guideline or the journal's instructions.

## Study-type decision tree

Start with the main design of the study or review.

### Is the study a synthesis of existing evidence?

For a **systematic review**, start with **PRISMA 2020** and check whether a more specific extension applies.

For a **scoping review**, use **PRISMA-ScR**.

For a **systematic review of diagnostic test accuracy**, use **PRISMA-DTA**.

Other types of reviews may have their own extensions or reporting guidelines. Search the EQUATOR Network when the review does not fit one of these categories.

### Is it a randomized controlled trial?

Use **CONSORT**. Check for an extension if the trial has a particular design, intervention, population, or other feature covered by one of the CONSORT extensions.

If participants were assigned to intervention groups without randomization, **TREND** may be applicable.

### Is it an observational study of human exposures or outcomes?

Examples include cohort, case-control, and cross-sectional studies.

Use **STROBE** and check whether a more specific extension applies.

### Is it a diagnostic accuracy study?

For a primary study evaluating the accuracy of a diagnostic test, use **STARD**.

If the study develops or validates a multivariable prediction model, **TRIPOD** may be more appropriate.

### Is it a case report?

Use **CARE**.

### Is it qualitative research?

**SRQR** provides general reporting guidance for qualitative research. **COREQ** is specifically designed for qualitative studies involving interviews and focus groups.

The appropriate choice depends on the study and the journal's requirements.

### Does the study involve animals in preclinical research?

Use **ARRIVE** and check the applicable version and study-specific guidance.

### Does the study develop or validate a prediction model?

Start with **TRIPOD** and check for an applicable extension or more specific prediction-model guidance.

### Is it an economic evaluation?

Check **CHEERS 2022**.

### Is it a quality-improvement study?

Check **SQUIRE**.

If none of these descriptions fits, search the EQUATOR Network by study design, methodology, or research area. A specialized guideline may exist even when the study does not fit one of the common categories above.

## PRISMA 2020, PRISMA-ScR, and PRISMA-DTA

The three PRISMA guidelines are used for different kinds of evidence synthesis.

| Feature                       | PRISMA 2020                                                    | PRISMA-ScR                                                | PRISMA-DTA                                            |
| ----------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Main use                      | Systematic reviews                                             | Scoping reviews                                           | Systematic reviews of diagnostic test accuracy        |
| Main purpose                  | Report how a systematic review was conducted and what it found | Report how evidence was identified, mapped, and described | Report systematic reviews of diagnostic test accuracy |
| Checklist                     | 27 items                                                       | 20 items                                                  | Diagnostic-accuracy-specific checklist                |
| Meta-analysis required        | No                                                             | No                                                        | No                                                    |
| Flow diagram                  | Yes                                                            | Yes                                                       | Yes                                                   |
| Diagnostic-specific reporting | No                                                             | No                                                        | Yes                                                   |

The distinction between PRISMA 2020 and PRISMA-ScR is not whether the review used a systematic search. A scoping review can use a systematic search and still requires PRISMA-ScR.

Likewise, a systematic review does not need to include a meta-analysis to use PRISMA 2020. The guideline covers the reporting of the review process and results whether or not statistical synthesis is performed.

PRISMA-DTA adds requirements relevant to diagnostic test accuracy, including the index test, reference standard, target condition, and measures such as sensitivity and specificity.

The current PRISMA materials should be used when completing the checklist rather than relying on a shortened version reproduced elsewhere.

## Mapping manuscript sections to checklist items

The following mapping is based on PRISMA 2020. It is useful for checking whether the information required by the checklist can be found in the manuscript, but individual items may span more than one section.

| Manuscript section | PRISMA 2020 items | What to look for                                                                                                                                         |
| ------------------ | ----------------: | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Title              |                 1 | Identifies the report as a systematic review                                                                                                             |
| Abstract           |                 2 | Contains the information required by the PRISMA 2020 abstract guidance                                                                                   |
| Introduction       |               3–4 | Rationale and objectives                                                                                                                                 |
| Methods            |              5–15 | Eligibility criteria, information sources, search strategy, selection, data collection, data items, risk of bias, effect measures, and synthesis methods |
| Results            |             16–22 | Study selection, study characteristics, risk of bias, results, synthesis, reporting biases, and certainty of evidence where applicable                   |
| Discussion         |             23–24 | Interpretation and limitations                                                                                                                           |
| Other information  |             25–27 | Registration/protocol, support, competing interests, and availability of data, code, or other materials where applicable                                 |

A checklist location should be specific enough that an editor or reviewer can find the relevant passage without searching the entire manuscript.

### Final check

Before submission, confirm that the selected guideline matches the study design, that applicable extensions have been considered, and that the completed checklist corresponds to the version of the guideline used for the manuscript.

## Reviewer and editorial quick check

* Does the selected guideline match the study design?
* Is there a more specific guideline or extension for this type of study?
* Is the checklist version current?
* Can the reported checklist items be located in the manuscript?
* Are the locations specific enough to be useful?
* If the selected guideline calls for a flow diagram, is the appropriate diagram provided?
* Are registration or protocol details reported where applicable?
* Are funding, competing interests, and data or materials availability reported as required?
* Are any items marked complete that cannot be located in the manuscript?
* Does the journal have additional reporting requirements?

A reporting checklist describes what should be reported; it does not by itself establish whether the underlying research was conducted correctly.

## Keeping the information current

Reporting guidelines and their extensions are updated over time. Before using a checklist for a new submission, check:

* the current EQUATOR Network database;
* the official site or publication for the selected guideline;
* any applicable extension; and
* the target journal's current author instructions.

PRISMA 2020, for example, replaced the original PRISMA 2009 statement. Other guidelines have also been revised or extended, so an old checklist saved from a previous submission should not automatically be reused.

If a study does not fit one of the common guidelines listed here, that is a reason to search further, not to default to a general checklist.
